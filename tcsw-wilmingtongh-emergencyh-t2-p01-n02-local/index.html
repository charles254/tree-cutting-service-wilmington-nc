How to Prepare Your Property for Tree Removal in Wilmington, North Carolina

Proper site preparation ensures tree removal operations proceed smoothly, efficiently, and without accidental damage to driveways, underground utilities, or delicate turf. Here is what Wilmington homeowners should do before tree crews arrive on site.

Access Route Preparation & Outdoor Clearing Checklist

Clearing driveways and access corridors before equipment arrives ensures operations proceed efficiently. Ground machinery like mini-skid steers and chippers requires minimum pathway clearances to move timber without contacting buildings or fences.

  • Park family vehicles along the street to leave wide driveway access for chip trucks
  • Move outdoor furniture, grills, planters, and garden hoses out of the work perimeter
  • Unlock side gates and verify gate openings meet equipment width minimums
  • Keep young children and household pets safely indoors throughout all tree cutting

Underground Utility Locating (811) & Landscape Protection Mats

Heavy tree equipment can damage buried utilities, septic drain fields, and irrigation piping if not identified beforehand. Dialing 811 marks public lines, while composite heavy-duty ground mats distribute machine axle weight to protect lawns and paver driveways.

  • Contact North Carolina 811 at least three business days prior to verify buried gas and electric lines
  • Flag underground sprinkler heads, valve boxes, and drip irrigation lines
  • Deploy heavy-duty composite mats across turf corridors to prevent tire rutting
  • Notify adjoining neighbors when trees grow along shared property boundaries

Frequently asked questions

How much gate clearance is required for tree removal machinery?

Standard commercial mini-loaders and motorized stump grinders typically require a minimum 36-to-48-inch gate opening to access fenced residential backyards safely.

Do I need to be present at home during tree removal?

While not strictly mandatory once outdoor access and written work orders are established, we recommend being present at the start to walk the property with our crew leader.

Checklist

  • Conduct a 360-degree visual inspection for trunk decay, dead canopy limbs, or root heaving.
  • Check clearance distances from overhead service lines, roof shingles, and neighboring fences.
  • Verify that your tree service provider carries active general liability and worker compensation certificates.
  • Confirm whether a City of Wilmington tree removal permit or arborist report is required.
  • Ensure the written estimate includes complete branch chipping, wood disposal, and ground raking.
